                Chicago, Friday, July 31, 1931.

                    Ten o'clock A. M.

            Court met pursuant to adjournment.

            PRESENT:  As before.

                    - - - - -

        THE COURT:  In the income tax cases, the motion
to withdraw the plea of guilty is granted.  As
the cases are to be tried, the interests of justice
require that there be no further comment upon the
proposed recommendation or the arrangement that was
attempted with reference to it.

            The case is set for trial on the 8th of
September.  There will be an order for a venire of
100 jurors.

            As to the conspiracy case, the ruling is
reserved, and the motion taken under advisement and
continued for disposition until September 8th.  The
matters covered by that indictment should be presented to another grand jury.  The enumeration
of overt acts the defendant and others are charged
with substantial offenses within the period of
the statute of limitations for which a heavier penalty
is provided than that possible under the conspiracy
indictment.  The court will not believe unless it
is forced to do so that the language of this in-
dictment was chosen without probable cause, merely
for the purpose of creating the impression that this
defendant had been a flagrant law breaker over a
long period of time.  This is a matter which must
be inquired into fully.

        The case will be set for trial in September.

        MR. JOHNSTON:  Your Honor, on one matter, the
Government has witnesses in several states.  We want
to try the case at an early date.  I have dis-
cussed that with the agents, and I think we would
almost need until the last week of September.  If
the court would give us that time, by the last week
of September we could be ready.
